\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We compare GA-LK with existing compilers in model fidelity, rearrangement batches, and rearrangement time. Controlled comparisons examine the roles of multilayer look-ahead and genetic search.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We use the 18 circuits evaluated in the ZAC paper, denoted ZAC18~\cite{lin2025zac}, and 154 input files from the MQT QMAP examples, denoted QMAP154~\cite{wille2023qmap}. QMAP154 contains 151 distinct circuits after normalization and deduplication. Equivalent inputs form one analysis unit, with fidelity averaged in the logarithmic domain. The external baselines are ZAC by Lin \etal~\cite{lin2025zac} and routing-aware placement by Stade \etal~\cite{stade2025routingaware}, denoted ICCAD/QMAP and implemented using MQT QMAP v3.2.0. All three compilers receive the same normalized inputs and gate sequences.

The hardware model comprises $100\times100$ storage-zone SLM traps, $7\times20$ entanglement-zone gate-site pairs, and a $100\times100$ AOD array. Fidelity follows~\eqref{eq:fidelity}, with $f_1=0.9997$, $f_2=0.995$, $f_{\rm exc}=0.9975$, $f_{\rm tran}=0.999$, and $T_2=1.5\times10^6\,\mu$s, matching the ZAC evaluation settings~\cite{lin2025zac}. Each SLM--AOD trap transfer contributes to $N_{\rm tran}$, and transport time contributes to $t_q$. The external baselines use their native outputs; GA-LK applies the candidate feasibility checks in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Each run has a 600~s time limit. Fidelity, batch counts, and rearrangement time are aggregated over distinct circuits for which all three compilers complete compilation and satisfy $t_q<T_2$. Complete-result counts include all input files.

The implementation is compiled in C++17 Release mode with OpenMP and fast-math optimizations disabled. Single-threaded timing uses a 14-core Apple M4 Pro with 24~GB memory, macOS~26.6.2, Python~3.10.19, and AppleClang~21.0.0.

The main comparison uses the solver budgets in Table~\ref{tab:solver-bounds} and $(H_{\max},\alpha,\rho,\epsilon)=(8,0.5,0.7,0.05)$. GA-LK runs with random seeds 0, 1, and 2, and the median is taken for each circuit. Parameters and circuit subsets for the controlled component comparisons are specified in their respective subsections.

\subsection{Compilation Quality}

GA-LK achieves higher geometric-mean model fidelity on both benchmark sets, with lower mean rearrangement batch counts and shorter mean rearrangement times than either baseline. Table~\ref{tab:main-results} summarizes the three methods. On ZAC18, the geometric mean of fidelity improves over ZAC and ICCAD/QMAP by \PaperPercentGain{\ZACMFourF}{\ZACMOneF} and \PaperPercentGain{\ZACMFourF}{\ZACMTwoF}, respectively. On QMAP154, the corresponding gains are \PaperPercentGain{\QMAPMFourF}{\QMAPMOneF} and \PaperPercentGain{\QMAPMFourF}{\QMAPMTwoF}, as plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:experimental-summary}(a).

Individual circuits obtain larger fidelity improvements. Among the four examples in Table~\ref{tab:representative-circuits}, the quantum Fourier transform circuit \texttt{QFT-18} has the largest relative gain. GA-LK also exceeds both baselines on the three Ising circuits; Fig.~\ref{fig:experimental-summary}(b) reports the per-circuit gains. Two examples are selected from each benchmark set. Eligible circuits have a maximum baseline fidelity of at least 0.05. They are ranked by GA-LK's log-fidelity difference from that maximum. The table reports circuit sizes and absolute fidelity, whereas the figure shows improvements over each baseline separately.

Fewer rearrangement batches mean fewer sequential stages of atom transport. On ZAC18, GA-LK reduces the mean batch count from \ZACMOneB{} for ZAC and \ZACMTwoB{} for ICCAD/QMAP to \ZACMFourB. On QMAP154, the corresponding counts decrease from \QMAPMOneB{} and \QMAPMTwoB{} to \QMAPMFourB. Mean rearrangement times decrease to \ZACMFourT\,ms and \QMAPMFourT\,ms, respectively, below both baselines. GA-LK thus reduces mean transport overhead in both batch count and duration.

\subsection{Multilayer Evaluation and Genetic Search}
\label{sec:ablation}

Including future-layer information with $H_{\max}=8$ improves the geometric mean of model fidelity over the zero-horizon configuration by \PaperPercentGain{\AblationHRatio}{1}. Under the same objective and evaluation budget, genetic search improves fidelity by \PaperPercentGain{\AblationGARatio}{1} over deterministic coordinate-wise greedy search with local refinement.

The horizon comparison uses $H_{\max}=8$ and $H_{\max}=0$ with shared $(\alpha,\rho,\epsilon)=(0.5,0.7,0.05)$, search budgets, and storage-site candidate domains. Both configurations satisfy the current layer's physical constraints. With a maximum horizon of eight layers, the configuration estimates future-layer physical costs and return costs at the window's end. It determines the residency-protection set from future reuse of the atoms. The zero-horizon configuration optimizes the current-layer cost. The genetic-versus-greedy comparison fixes the $H_{\max}=8$ objective and all physical constraints.

These comparisons use all ZAC18 circuits and 30 QMAP154 circuits selected by stratification. QMAP154 is divided into three strata by two-qubit gate count: at most 300, 301--1500, and above 1500, with ten circuits selected per stratum. Selection follows the hash order of normalized circuit contents within each stratum; the circuit lists are available in the code repository. The horizon and search-strategy comparisons include \AblationHN{} and \AblationGAN{} circuits, respectively, with complete results for all three seeds.

\subsection{Parameter Selection and Compilation Cost}

Candidate count and look-ahead depth determine search computation. Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} reports how evaluation budget, storage-site domain, maximum horizon, and decay weights affect fidelity and compilation time. The fixed 12-circuit subset contains two circuits per stratum, selected by the same hash-order rule. ZAC18 uses three qubit-count strata: at most 32, 33--64, and above 64; QMAP154 retains the two-qubit-gate strata.

Among the evaluated configurations, smaller evaluation budgets and storage-site domains retain near-default fidelity, with respective compilation-time ratios of \SensitivityBudgetLowTimeRatio{} and \SensitivityReturnSmallTimeRatio. A maximum horizon of four layers also retains near-default fidelity.

Candidate search and future-state evaluation increase GA-LK's compilation cost. On the fixed 12-circuit subset, the median end-to-end compilation time is \StrictMFourTime\,s; the geometric mean of per-circuit time ratios to ICCAD/QMAP is \StrictMFourVsMTwo. Each compiler uses its native single-threaded implementation; timing is the median of three post-warm-up runs.
