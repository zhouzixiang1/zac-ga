\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The evaluation uses 18 ZAC benchmarks, denoted ZAC18~\cite{lin2025zac}, and 154 MQT QMAP example files, denoted QMAP154~\cite{wille2023qmap}. QMAP154 contains 151 distinct circuits after normalization; equivalent inputs are combined, with fidelity averaged in the logarithmic domain. The baselines are ZAC by Lin \etal~\cite{lin2025zac} and routing-aware placement by Stade \etal~\cite{stade2025routingaware}. The latter is denoted ICCAD/QMAP and uses the MQT QMAP v3.2.0 implementation. All three compilers receive the same normalized inputs and gate sequences.

The hardware comprises $100\times100$ storage-zone SLM traps, $7\times20$ entanglement-zone gate-site pairs, and a $100\times100$ AOD array. Equation~\eqref{eq:fidelity} uses $f_1=0.9997$, $f_2=0.995$, $f_{\rm exc}=0.9975$, $f_{\rm tran}=0.999$, and $T_2=1.5\times10^6\,\mu$s~\cite{lin2025zac}. Trap transfers are counted per atom, and transport time contributes to coherence loss. Baselines are scored from their native outputs; GA-LK applies the candidate feasibility checks in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Each compilation has a 600~s limit. Aggregate quality comparisons include distinct circuits for which all three methods complete compilation and satisfy $t_q<T_2$; complete-result counts cover all input files.

GA-LK uses the solver budgets in Table~\ref{tab:solver-bounds}, an eight-layer maximum horizon, and look-ahead parameters $(\alpha,\rho,\epsilon)=(0.5,0.7,0.05)$. Each circuit is run with random seeds 0, 1, and 2, and the median result is reported. The native kernel is compiled in C++17 Release mode with OpenMP and fast-math disabled. The platform comprises a 14-core Apple M4 Pro, 24~GB memory, and macOS~26.6.2, using Python~3.10.19 and AppleClang~21.0.0. All compilers run in a single thread throughout the evaluation.

\subsection{Overall Compilation Quality}

GA-LK achieved higher geometric-mean fidelity, fewer mean rearrangement batches, and shorter mean rearrangement times on both benchmark sets, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:main-results}. Fidelity improved over ZAC and ICCAD/QMAP by \PaperPercentGain{\ZACMFourF}{\ZACMOneF} and \PaperPercentGain{\ZACMFourF}{\ZACMTwoF}, respectively, on ZAC18. The corresponding gains on QMAP154 were \PaperPercentGain{\QMAPMFourF}{\QMAPMOneF} and \PaperPercentGain{\QMAPMFourF}{\QMAPMTwoF}. The gains on both benchmark sets are plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:experimental-summary}(a).


On ZAC18, the mean rearrangement batch count decreased from \ZACMOneB{} for ZAC and \ZACMTwoB{} for ICCAD/QMAP to \ZACMFourB. On QMAP154, it decreased from \QMAPMOneB{} and \QMAPMTwoB{} to \QMAPMFourB, respectively. Mean rearrangement times fell to \ZACMFourT\,ms and \QMAPMFourT\,ms, respectively, below both baselines. These reductions indicate fewer sequential transport stages and less total time required to complete them.

\subsection{Representative Circuit Comparisons}

Quantum Fourier transform and Ising circuits illustrate GA-LK's fidelity advantage on individual workloads. Among these examples, \texttt{QFT-18} had the largest relative gain, and all three Ising instances also exceeded both baselines. Two examples are selected from each benchmark set, requiring the higher baseline fidelity to be at least 0.05. Eligible circuits are then ranked by GA-LK's log-fidelity improvement over that value.

\subsection{Multilayer Evaluation and Genetic Search}
\label{sec:ablation}

Multilayer look-ahead accounts for how current placement affects subsequent gate layers. In a controlled comparison of \AblationHN{} circuits, an eight-layer maximum horizon improved geometric-mean fidelity over a zero horizon by \PaperPercentGain{\AblationHRatio}{1}. A separate comparison fixed the objective and evaluation budget across search strategies. Genetic search improved fidelity by \PaperPercentGain{\AblationGARatio}{1} over deterministic coordinate-wise greedy search with local refinement on \AblationGAN{} circuits.

The horizon comparison changes only $H_{\max}$, keeping all other parameters, search budgets, and storage-site candidate domains fixed. The eight-layer configuration includes future-layer costs, return costs at the window's end, and residency protection determined by future reuse. The zero-horizon configuration instead uses the current-layer cost as its objective. The search comparison fixes the eight-layer horizon and all physical constraints, replacing only the search strategy for large boundaries.

Both comparisons use circuits with complete three-seed results from all ZAC18 circuits and a 30-circuit QMAP154 subset. The QMAP154 subset contains ten circuits from each two-qubit-gate-count stratum: at most 300, 301--1500, and above 1500. The stratified selection rule and complete circuit lists are available in the code repository.

\subsection{Look-Ahead for Initial Placement}

The preceding GA-LK main experiments and ablations retain layer-weighted simulated-annealing initialization. We separately evaluate the extension in Section~\ref{sec:initial-placement} on \InitStudyN{} circuits covering different algorithm families and sizes. The comparison uses $H_{\rm init}=0$ and $H_{\rm init}=2$ on the same candidate pool, with an eight-layer horizon for subsequent dynamic placement in both cases. The original simulated-annealing mapping provides an additional reference.

Log fidelity and batch counts are first averaged over three seeds within each circuit, then aggregated over complete circuits. With \InitTimeouts{} Ising paired run exceeding 600~s, \InitCompleteN{} complete circuits enter the paired comparison. Two-layer initial look-ahead increases geometric-mean fidelity by \InitFidelityGainRounded{} and reduces mean batches by \InitBatchReductionRounded{} relative to first-layer evaluation; its fidelity increase over the original simulated-annealing initialization is \InitVsSAGainRounded. Initialization gains are small on this sample. The main comparison continues to evaluate dynamic joint optimization under the original initializer. Complete per-circuit results and the timeout record are available in the code repository.
