\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Inter-layer residency avoids round trips through storage and reduces trap transfers. Resident atoms continue occupying entanglement-zone gate sites and undergo nontarget Rydberg excitation during intermediate layers. Returning atoms avoid these pulses, but their storage sites affect other trajectories. GA-LK compares residency and return choices through trap transfers, idle-atom excitation, and per-atom coherence losses.

Return storage sites affect subsequent trajectory feasibility. Both storage-site choices in Fig.~\ref{fig:baseline-motivation} require the same trap-transfer count, but an atom on the path blocks subsequent direct transport. GA-LK preserves the resulting atom positions, occupancy, and clocks, and evaluates future layers from these candidate-specific states. Candidates with equal current costs receive different evaluations when subsequent transport distances differ, as in Fig.~\ref{fig:physical-lookahead}.

ZAC reuses atoms through adjacent-layer matching and guides dynamic placement with next-partner distances~\cite{lin2025zac}. ICCAD/QMAP incorporates current movement compatibility and the longest within-group trajectory into its routing-aware cost~\cite{stade2025routingaware}. GA-LK jointly selects physically feasible gate sites, residency, and storage sites, evaluating their effects across multiple subsequent layers. The finite-horizon configuration improves model fidelity in the controlled comparisons. Genetic search also outperforms coordinate-wise greedy search under the same objective and budget.

The rearrangement batch count measures sequential transport stages, while rearrangement time measures their total duration. AOD constraints and current occupancy determine trajectory compatibility. Graph coloring and occupancy-order checks convert spatial placements into executable batches. GA-LK achieves lower mean batch counts and shorter mean rearrangement times than both baselines on both benchmark sets, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:main-results}. The quantum Fourier transform and Ising circuits in Fig.~\ref{fig:experimental-summary} also exhibit substantial fidelity improvements.

The evaluation budget controls search size, while the maximum horizon bounds each candidate's future-layer evaluation. Decay weights control how distant interactions influence the current decision. In the sensitivity comparisons, smaller evaluation budgets and storage-site domains reduce compilation time while retaining model fidelity close to the default configuration. All configurations use the same physical feasibility constraints.
